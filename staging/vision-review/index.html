Homepage · Vision & Thesis

Four ways to set the thesis.

The first attempt reproduced the reference layout rather than designing from it. These four are designed: same words, four different arguments about what the section is for. All use the site\'s own palette and type, so what you see is what would ship.

A

Pillars

The three sectors, given equal weight

The statement sits on top, then the three sectors stand as equal columns under heavy rules, each with one line saying what it means in practice. The qualities run underneath as a quiet third row. This is the most conventional of the four, in the good sense: it is how a fund with a clear sector thesis usually says so.

Argues for

A founder knows in two seconds whether they are in scope. The three sectors are the message, and nothing competes with them.

Argues against

Three equal columns is the most familiar layout on any fund site. It is safe rather than distinctive.

B

Ledger

The thesis as a record, label beside position

No boxes and no columns of equal weight. A narrow rail carries the statement, and the thesis runs down the page as four labelled rows: where we invest, what we look for, who we back, how we accelerate. It reads like a document rather than a web page, which is the register LPs are used to.

The most institutional of the four, and the easiest to extend: a fifth position is one more row, not a broken grid.

Argues against

The left rail runs shorter than the right, so there is real whitespace to live with. Deliberate, but some people read it as unfinished.

C

Fact sheet

A large statement beside a compact fund sheet

The vision is given real scale on the left, and everything operational is compressed into a bordered sheet on the right: stage, sectors, markets, what we look for, founders, what we bring. The contrast of scale does the work. It is the closest thing on the page to a one-pager a GP would hand over.

D

Criteria

Written for the founder reading it

The statement runs across the top, then two columns answer the only two questions a founder actually has: what we look for, and what we bring. Each point gets a short gloss, so the section says something rather than listing attributes. The one option written outward rather than inward.

Recommendation

B if this is for LPs. D if it is for founders.

Ledger is the one that looks like a fund rather than a website. It is quiet, it extends cleanly, and it puts the thesis on the record without dressing it up. If the homepage is doing work in a raise, this is the one.

Criteria is the one a founder will actually read, because it answers their question instead of describing the fund. If inbound quality matters more than institutional signalling, take D.

Pillars is the safe answer and nobody will object to it. Fact sheet is the most useful but the most maintenance, and it introduces the only bordered box on the page.

Whichever you pick, the copy can move between them without redesigning, so choosing the shape is the only decision here.
